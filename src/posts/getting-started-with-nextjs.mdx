If you just built a custom PC, installed Windows, and immediately started downloading games, you are likely leaving a massive amount of performance on the table.

Before you launch into your first match, you need to dive into the BIOS. Here is exactly what you need to configure to push your hardware to its actual limits.

## 1. Enable XMP/EXPO for RAM Overclocking

By default, high-speed DDR4 or DDR5 RAM will run at standard base speeds (usually 2133MHz or 4800MHz) regardless of what is printed on the box.

To get the memory frequency you actually paid for:

- Reboot and hit **DEL** or **F2** to enter BIOS.
- Locate the **Extreme Memory Profile (XMP)** or **EXPO** setting.
- Change it from Disabled to **Profile 1**.
- _Note: If you want to manually tighten your RAM timings for better 1% low framerates in competitive titles like Apex Legends, we will cover advanced memory overclocking in a future guide._

## 2. Enable Re-Size BAR (Smart Access Memory)

Re-Size BAR allows your CPU to access the entirety of your GPU's VRAM at once, rather than in small chunks. This can provide a noticeable FPS boost in many modern titles.

1. Navigate to your BIOS **Advanced** or **PCIe Subsystem Settings**.
2. Enable **Above 4G Decoding**.
3. Set **Re-Size BAR Support** to Auto or Enabled.

## Testing the Results

Once you save your BIOS settings and boot into Windows, fire up a hardware monitoring tool like HWiNFO64 to verify your memory frequency. Run a benchmark in a visually demanding engine like Red Dead Redemption 2 to ensure your system remains perfectly stable under load.
